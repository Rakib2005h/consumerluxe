How to Spot a Real Deal — Not Just a Big Discount

August 2026 · Consumer Luxe Editorial

That "-72%" badge feels exciting. It's also, very often, fiction. Strikethrough "list prices" are set by sellers, and some of them are inflated precisely so the discount looks dramatic. After verifying hundreds of listings for our weekly edit, here's the framework we actually use — and you can too, in about a minute per product.

1. Never trust the strikethrough alone

A genuine list price (Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price) is real, but many marketplace listings show a "was" price that the item rarely or never sold for. The bigger the claimed percentage, the more suspicious you should be.

2. Check the price history

Price trackers like camelcamelcamel (for Amazon) record every price change going back years. You instantly see whether today's price is actually low for that item — or just mid-range wearing a discount costume. This is exactly how we verify every price we publish: each listing on Consumer Luxe carries a "price checked" date from tracker data, and when the current price is higher than the historical list, we show only the current price and no fake savings.

3. Beware the anchor-and-release cycle

A common pattern: a product sits at $89.99 for months, jumps to $129.99 for two weeks, then "drops" to $94.99 as a deal. The tracker chart makes this obvious — a steady line is trustworthy; a sudden spike right before a sale is a red flag.

4. Unit price beats total price

Multi-packs are where value hides. A 3-pack of socks at $24.99 sounds mediocre until you see it's $8.33/pair against $12 for a single pair elsewhere. We flag genuine multi-pack value in our fashion picks for exactly this reason.

5. Check whether the listing is alive

Dead variants — a size or colour that no longer has any seller — are everywhere. If a link shows "currently unavailable," the deal was never real for you. We mark such listings honestly with "Check Price on Amazon" instead of inventing a number.

The 60-second checklist:
  1. Open the price-history chart for the item.
  2. Compare today's price to the 12-month average and all-time low.
  3. Verify the "list price" is a real MSRP, not a two-week-old spike.
  4. Compute the per-unit price if it's a pack.
  5. Confirm your size/colour variant is actually in stock.

What we do differently

Every card on Consumer Luxe shows the price with its check date, real product images from the listing, and no invented numbers. If a product's price can't be verified, you'll see "Check Price on Amazon" — that's honesty, not laziness.
